# 02 — From AI Assistance to AI-Native Workflows

## Learning objectives

- Recognize the difference between isolated AI usage and redesigned workflows.
- Assess workflow maturity without equating tool adoption with business impact.
- Select a safe first workflow for a team.

## The distinction

**AI-assisted:** a developer asks an assistant to explain code, generate a function, or draft tests. The existing process remains mostly unchanged.

**AI-native:** the workflow is designed around collaboration between people, tools, and bounded agents. Work is specified, context is reusable, steps are orchestrated, and output is verified.

AI-native does not mean human-free. It means intentional allocation of work and decisions.

## A practical maturity scale

| Level | Description | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 0 — Ad hoc | Individual experimentation | Inconsistent usage |
| 1 — Assisted | AI helps with isolated tasks | Personal prompts and suggestions |
| 2 — Repeatable | Shared patterns and guidance | Team templates and conventions |
| 3 — Orchestrated | Multi-step workflows with verification | Agent workflows and automated checks |
| 4 — Bounded autonomy | Agents execute defined tasks within guardrails | Permission boundaries, telemetry, escalation |

This is a discussion model, not an industry certification or universal standard.

## Assessment exercise

Choose one recurring engineering activity. Score it against:

- Repeatability of inputs
- Availability of context
- Verifiability of outputs
- Risk of incorrect action
- Reversibility
- Human approval requirements

Select a workflow with clear success criteria, low blast radius, and fast feedback.

## Expected outcome

A baseline maturity assessment and one prioritized pilot.
